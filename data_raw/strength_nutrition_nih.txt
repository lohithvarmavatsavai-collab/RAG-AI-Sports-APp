SOURCE: Dietary Protein and Muscle Synthesis - NIH Overview (2023)
ORGANIZATION: National Institutes of Health (NIH) - Office of Dietary Supplements
CATEGORY: Strength Training - Nutrition
------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
The NIH provides guidance on dietary protein requirements for active individuals, distinguishing between the needs of sedentary adults and those engaged in intense athletic training.

PROTEIN QUALITY AND AMINO ACIDS
- Proteins are made up of 20 amino acids. Nine of these are essential amino acids (EAAs) that the body cannot produce and must be obtained from food.
- Complete proteins contain all nine EAAs. Animal sources (meat, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy) are generally complete proteins.
- Incomplete proteins lack one or more EAAs. Most plant sources (beans, grains, nuts) are incomplete, though soy and quinoa are notable exceptions.

SUPPLEMENTATION vs. WHOLE FOODS
- Protein powders (whey, casein, pea, soy) offer a convenient and efficient way to increase protein intake, particularly around workout times.
- However, whole food protein sources provide additional essential nutrients (e.g., iron, zinc, B vitamins) that are not typically found in isolated protein supplements.
- The NIH recommends prioritizing whole foods and using supplements only when daily targets cannot be met through diet alone.

SAFETY OF HIGH PROTEIN DIETS
- For healthy individuals with normal kidney function, consuming a high-protein diet (up to 2.0-2.5 g/kg/day) does not cause kidney damage or adverse health effects.
- Individuals with pre-existing kidney disease should consult a physician before increasing protein intake.

CARBOHYDRATES FOR STRENGTH TRAINING
- While protein builds muscle, carbohydrates fuel the workouts.
- Consuming carbohydrates alongside protein post-workout can stimulate insulin release, which may further enhance the muscle-building environment, though protein is the primary driver.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Prioritize complete, whole-food protein sources.
- Use protein supplements for convenience, not as a replacement for a balanced diet.
- High protein diets are safe for healthy individuals.
