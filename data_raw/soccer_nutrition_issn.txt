ORGANIZATION: International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN)
CATEGORY: Soccer - Nutrition

OVERVIEW
The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) position stand on carbohydrates and exercise performance confirms that carbohydrate availability is one of the most important nutritional factors for intermittent high-intensity sports like soccer. This document summarizes the key ISSN positions as applied to soccer athletes.

KEY ISSN POSITIONS ON CARBOHYDRATES

1. Carbohydrates Are Essential for High-Intensity Sport
   - Muscle glycogen is the primary fuel during high-intensity efforts (sprinting, pressing, defending)
   - Low glycogen impairs both physical performance and decision-making
   - Soccer players who start a match with depleted glycogen perform significantly worse in the second half

2. Recommended Daily Carbohydrate Intake for Soccer
   - Light training day: 3–5 g/kg body weight
   - Moderate training day: 5–7 g/kg body weight
   - High-intensity training or match day: 6–10 g/kg body weight
   - Example: A 70 kg player should consume 420–700 g of carbohydrates on a match day

3. Carbohydrate Loading Before Competition
   - For matches or tournaments lasting 90+ minutes, carbohydrate loading in the 24–48 hours prior is beneficial
   - Strategy: Increase carbohydrate intake to 8–12 g/kg body weight while reducing training intensity
   - Glycogen stores can increase by 10–20% with proper loading

4. Intra-Match Carbohydrate Use
   - Consuming 30–60 g/hr of carbohydrate during a match maintains blood glucose and delays fatigue
   - Sources: sports drinks (6–8% carbohydrate concentration), energy gels, banana pieces
   - Mouth rinsing with carbohydrate solution (without swallowing) may also improve performance when full intake is not possible

5. Post-Match Glycogen Replenishment
   - Immediate replenishment (within 30 minutes) is most efficient
   - Rate: 1.0–1.5 g/kg body weight in the first hour
   - Combine with 20 g of protein to enhance glycogen synthesis and begin muscle repair

FOOD SOURCES OF CARBOHYDRATES
- Fast-digesting (good pre/during/post match): white rice, white bread, bananas, sports drinks, energy gels, fruit juice
- Slow-digesting (good for daily intake): oatmeal, whole grain pasta, sweet potatoes, legumes, brown rice

PRACTICAL APPLICATIONS
- Beginner players often underestimate carbohydrate needs
- Do not restrict carbohydrates in an attempt to lose weight during a competitive season
- Distribute carbohydrate intake throughout the day; do not eat all in one meal
- Plan post-training nutrition as seriously as pre-training nutrition

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
- "Carbs make you fat" — FALSE for athletes in active training; carbs fuel performance
- "You only need carbs on game day" — FALSE; daily carbohydrate intake supports training adaptations
- "Protein is more important than carbs for athletes" — INCORRECT for intermittent sports; carbs are primary fuel

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Match-day carbohydrate intake should be 6–10 g/kg body weight
- Carbohydrate loading 24–48 hrs before competition can improve performance
- Intra-match carbohydrate intake (30–60 g/hr) delays fatigue
- Post-match recovery window is 30–45 minutes for optimal replenishment
