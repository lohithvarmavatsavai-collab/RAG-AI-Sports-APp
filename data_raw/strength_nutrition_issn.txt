SOURCE: Protein Requirements for Strength Athletes - ISSN Position Stand (2022)
ORGANIZATION: International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN)
CATEGORY: Strength Training - Nutrition
------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
The ISSN provides a comprehensive review of protein needs for individuals engaged in regular resistance training, aiming to maximize muscle protein synthesis (MPS) and promote hypertrophy.

DAILY PROTEIN INTAKE
- To build and maintain muscle mass, an overall daily protein intake in the range of 1.4-2.0 g protein/kg body weight/day (g/kg/d) is sufficient for most exercising individuals.
- Higher intakes (2.3-3.1 g/kg/d) may be needed during periods of caloric restriction (cutting) to prevent the loss of lean muscle mass.

PER-MEAL PROTEIN DOSING
- To maximize muscle protein synthesis, protein should be evenly distributed across 3-4 meals throughout the day.
- Optimal per-meal dose: 0.25-0.40 g/kg/meal, or an absolute dose of 20-40 g of protein per meal.
- The protein source should contain a high amount of essential amino acids (EAAs), particularly leucine (700-3000 mg per dose), which acts as the primary trigger for MPS.

PROTEIN TIMING AND SOURCES
- The "anabolic window" of opportunity post-exercise is wider than previously thought. However, consuming high-quality protein within 2 hours after a workout is a prudent strategy.
- High-quality sources: Whey, casein, eggs, meat, poultry, dairy.
- Plant-based athletes: Can achieve similar results by combining protein sources (e.g., rice and pea protein) or consuming slightly larger total amounts of protein to ensure adequate EAA intake.

PRE-SLEEP PROTEIN
- Consuming 30-40 g of slow-digesting protein (like casein, found in dairy) 30 minutes before sleep can increase overnight muscle protein synthesis and improve recovery.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Consume 1.4-2.0 g/kg/d of protein daily.
- Spread protein intake evenly across 3-4 meals (20-40g per meal).
- Consider 30-40g of casein protein before bed to enhance overnight recovery.
