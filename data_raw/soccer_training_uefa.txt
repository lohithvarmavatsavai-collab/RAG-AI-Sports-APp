SOURCE: UEFA Football Performance Program - Conditioning Guidelines (2022)
ORGANIZATION: Union of European Football Associations (UEFA)
CATEGORY: Soccer - Training
------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
UEFA's coaching and performance program establishes conditioning benchmarks and training structures for amateur and club-level football players. The program emphasizes integrated training — combining physical, technical, and mental components rather than training them in isolation.

PHYSICAL PERFORMANCE BENCHMARKS (Amateur/Intermediate Level)
- A mid-level amateur player covers 8–10 km per match
- High-intensity runs account for 1–2 km of that distance
- Sprint speed targets: 25–28 km/h achievable for recreational players

CONDITIONING COMPONENTS
1. Aerobic Endurance
   - Foundation for all soccer actions
   - Methods: continuous runs at 65–75% HRmax, Yo-Yo Intermittent Recovery Test used for assessment
   - Recommended: 2 aerobic-focused sessions per week during pre-season

2. Anaerobic Power / High-Intensity Efforts
   - Soccer involves repeated short bursts (5–15 m)
   - Training: interval sprints, repeated sprint ability (RSA) drills
   - RSA protocol: 6–10 x 30m sprints with 30-second recovery

3. Strength and Power
   - Lower body: squat, single-leg press, hip thrust
   - Core: planks, rotational strength for kicking and shielding
   - Upper body: push/pull for physical duels

4. Coordination and Agility
   - Cone drills, ladder drills, reactive agility with decision trigger (light or sound cue)

TRAINING LOAD MANAGEMENT
- UEFA recommends monitoring training load using RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion) scale
- Beginner: keep weekly load moderate; avoid sudden spikes (>10% week-to-week increase)
- Intermediate: can sustain higher volume; monitor fatigue signs

INTEGRATED TRAINING SESSION STRUCTURE
A standard UEFA-aligned session follows this flow:
1. Warm-up (FIFA 11+ or UEFA equivalent) — 15 min
2. Technical block — 20 min
3. Physical conditioning or SSG — 25 min
4. Tactical game or set piece — 15 min
5. Cool-down and stretching — 10 min

MENTAL AND TACTICAL COMPONENTS
- UEFA emphasizes that physical training alone is insufficient
- Position-specific conditioning (e.g., wingers need more sprint training than central defenders)
- Decision-making under physical fatigue is a trainable skill

PLAYER DEVELOPMENT FRAMEWORK
- Beginner stage: Learn basic movement patterns and rules; low-intensity conditioning
- Intermediate stage: Build aerobic base; introduce structured conditioning
- Advanced stage: Periodized training, sport-science monitoring

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Physical fitness and technical skill develop best together
- Monitor load and avoid overtraining
- Use RSA drills to specifically train repeated sprint ability
- Match demands should guide practice design
