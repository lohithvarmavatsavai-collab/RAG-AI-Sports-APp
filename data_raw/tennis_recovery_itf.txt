SOURCE: Cooling and Hydration Strategies in Hot-weather Tennis (2023)
ORGANIZATION: ITF Sports Science Committee
CATEGORY: Tennis - Recovery
------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
Playing tennis in hot environments increases physiological strain, sweat rates, and the risk of heat illness. The ITF provides specific guidelines for managing heat stress through hydration and cooling strategies.

PRE-COOLING
- Lowering body temperature before the match can delay fatigue.
- Strategies: Ingesting ice slurry drinks, wearing ice vests during the warm-up, or applying cold towels to the neck/head.

PER-COOLING (During Match)
- Using the 90-second changeovers effectively is critical.
- Ice towels draped around the neck and over the head.
- Drinking cold fluids (0-5°C).
- Seeking shade using umbrellas.

HYDRATION IN THE HEAT
- Thirst is not an adequate indicator of hydration status during play.
- Players should weigh themselves pre- and post-practice to calculate specific sweat rates.
- Add electrolytes (specifically sodium) to drinks to promote fluid retention.
- If a player loses >2% body weight, they started the match under-hydrated or drank insufficiently.

RECOVERY POST-MATCH IN HEAT
- Rapidly cool the body using cold water immersion (ice baths).
- Aggressively rehydrate with 150% of weight lost.
- Avoid further heat exposure for the rest of the day.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Use ice towels and cold drinks during changeovers in hot weather.
- Pre-cooling before the match can improve performance in extreme heat.
- Monitor hydration status by weighing in before and after matches.
