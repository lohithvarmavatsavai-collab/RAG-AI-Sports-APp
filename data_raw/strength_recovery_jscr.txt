SOURCE: Rest Periods and Deload Strategies in Resistance Training (2022)
ORGANIZATION: Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research (JSCR)
CATEGORY: Strength Training - Recovery
------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
Continuous, high-intensity resistance training without planned periods of reduced volume or intensity leads to overtraining, injury, and stalled progress. The JSCR reviews the efficacy of deload weeks and rest interval management.

DELOAD WEEKS
- A deload is a planned period of reduced training stress, typically lasting one week.
- Purpose: To allow accumulated fatigue to dissipate while maintaining fitness, leading to a "supercompensation" effect where strength levels jump up.
- Frequency: Most trainees benefit from a deload week every 4 to 8 weeks, depending on training intensity and individual recovery capacity.
- How to Deload:
  1. Volume reduction: Cut the number of sets and reps in half, keep weight the same.
  2. Intensity reduction: Keep sets and reps the same, but reduce weight by 40-50%.
  3. Both: A slight reduction in both volume and intensity.

REST INTERVALS BETWEEN SETS
- The time spent resting between sets directly impacts the training outcome.
- Hypertrophy (Muscle Growth): 60-90 seconds rest. This maximizes metabolic stress and hormonal response.
- Maximal Strength: 3-5 minutes rest. This allows for near-complete recovery of the ATP-PC system, enabling maximal force production in the subsequent set.
- Beginners should lean towards 90-120 seconds to ensure proper form is maintained throughout the workout.

ACTIVE RECOVERY FOR LIFTERS
- On non-lifting days, engaging in light, low-impact aerobic work (e.g., brisk walking, easy cycling) can aid recovery by promoting blood flow and nutrient delivery to recovering muscles without inducing further muscle damage.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Implement a deload week every 4-8 weeks to prevent overtraining.
- Rest 3-5 minutes between heavy strength sets, and 60-90 seconds for hypertrophy sets.
- Use light walking or cycling on rest days to promote blood flow.
