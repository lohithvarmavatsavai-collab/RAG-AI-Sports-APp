CATEGORY: Tennis - Recovery
------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
The ITF Medical Commission integrates sleep science, injury epidemiology, and long-term athlete development (LTAD) principles into recovery recommendations. Overuse injuries and burnout are the two primary long-term risks for tennis players, and both are directly tied to poor recovery management.

SLEEP SCIENCE FOR TENNIS ATHLETES
1. Sleep Architecture and Athletic Recovery
   - Deep sleep (slow-wave sleep) is when Growth Hormone (GH) is secreted — critical for tissue repair.
   - REM sleep consolidates skill learning and tactical memory. Tennis players who sleep < 7 hours show measurably worse decision-making under pressure.
   - Recommended sleep: 9 hours for junior players (< 18 years); 8–9 hours for adults.

2. Sleep Hygiene Protocols
   - Avoid blue light (screens) 90 minutes before bed.
   - Room temperature should be 18–20°C (cool promotes sleep onset).
   - Consistent bedtime and wake time even on rest days (within 30-minute window).
   - Post-match arousal (adrenaline, cortisol) delays sleep onset — use breathing techniques (4-7-8 method) or light reading to decelerate.

3. Napping Guidelines
   - A 20-minute nap before 3 PM improves afternoon alertness and reaction time.
   - Naps longer than 30 minutes risk sleep inertia (grogginess).
   - Strategic napping before evening matches can compensate for disrupted nighttime sleep.

OVERUSE INJURY PREVENTION IN TENNIS
Common overuse injuries: Lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow), rotator cuff tendinopathy, patellar tendinopathy, stress fractures.

1. Elbow and Shoulder
   - Forearm strengthening: Wrist curls, reverse wrist curls, pronation/supination with light dumbbell.
   - Rotator cuff maintenance: Sidelying external rotation, prone Y-T-W, face pulls.
   - Minimum 2 sessions/week year-round regardless of season.

2. Knee
   - Eccentric quadriceps strengthening (decline squats) prevents patellar tendinopathy.
   - Landing mechanics training: Soft knees during split-step landing, never land stiff-legged.

3. Back
   - Core anti-rotation (Pallof press, dead bugs) protects lumbar spine from rotational forces of serving.
   - Thoracic mobility daily: Cat-cow, thoracic rotations, foam roller thoracic extension.

LONG-TERM ATHLETE DEVELOPMENT (LTAD) — RECOVERY FOCUS
- Junior athletes (12–16): Prioritize sleep and nutrition over any recovery modality. 10 hours sleep recommended.
- Late adolescents (16–21): Recovery modalities (CWI, massage) can be introduced alongside sleep priority.
- Adults: Full recovery stack can be implemented — CWI, compression, nutrition timing, sleep extension.

MENTAL RECOVERY
- Match loss analysis should be limited to 30 minutes, then deliberately stopped.
- Journaling, music, or social connection should follow match reflection.
- Chronic performance anxiety is a recovery issue — breathing-based interventions (diaphragmatic breathing) reduce cortisol and accelerate nervous system recovery.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Sleep 8–9 hours, prioritize consistent schedule, and avoid screens before bed.
- Rotator cuff and forearm strengthening must be done year-round to prevent overuse injuries.
- Use 20-minute naps before afternoon matches to boost alertness.
- Limit post-match analysis to 30 minutes to protect mental recovery.
