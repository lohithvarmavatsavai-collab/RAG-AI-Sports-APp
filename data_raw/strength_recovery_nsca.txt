SOURCE: Sleep and Recovery for Strength Athletes - Consensus Review (2022)
ORGANIZATION: National Strength and Conditioning Association (NSCA)
CATEGORY: Strength Training - Recovery
------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
Recovery from heavy resistance training involves repairing muscle tissue, replenishing glycogen stores, and allowing the central nervous system (CNS) to recuperate. The NSCA highlights sleep as the paramount recovery strategy.

SLEEP AND HORMONE PRODUCTION
- The majority of Human Growth Hormone (HGH) and testosterone are secreted during deep sleep (slow-wave sleep).
- These hormones are critical for muscle hypertrophy and tissue repair.
- Sleep deprivation (getting less than 6-7 hours per night) significantly blunts the release of these anabolic hormones and increases cortisol (a catabolic, stress hormone), creating an unfavorable environment for muscle growth.

MANAGING CENTRAL NERVOUS SYSTEM (CNS) FATIGUE
- Heavy compound lifts (e.g., deadlifts, heavy squats) tax the CNS heavily.
- Symptoms of CNS fatigue include decreased grip strength, lack of motivation, disrupted sleep, and stalled progress.
- Recovery from CNS fatigue takes longer than localized muscle recovery; it may take 48-72 hours for the CNS to fully recover from a maximal lifting session.

NUTRITION AS RECOVERY
- Recovery is impossible without adequate caloric intake.
- To build muscle, a slight caloric surplus (200-500 extra calories per day) is generally required, alongside adequate protein.

HYDRATION FOR STRENGTH
- Dehydration reduces maximal strength and power output.
- Muscle cells that are well-hydrated are more anabolic (prone to growth).
- Aim for clear or pale-yellow urine daily.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Sleep 8+ hours to maximize growth hormone and testosterone production.
- Recognize that heavy lifts require CNS recovery, not just muscle recovery.
- Ensure adequate overall caloric intake to support the recovery process.
