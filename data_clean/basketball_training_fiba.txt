SOURCE: FIBA Basketball Athletic Development Program (2023)
ORGANIZATION: International Basketball Federation (FIBA)
CATEGORY: Basketball - Training
------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
FIBA's physical development guidelines for basketball focus on the specific demands of the sport: explosive jumping, rapid changes of direction, and repeated sprinting, all interspersed with lower-intensity periods of play.

TRAINING PRIORITIES
1. Power and Explosiveness: Vertical jump height is critical for rebounding and shooting. Plyometric training should be a staple.
2. Agility and Quickness: Defensive slides and offensive drives require lateral quickness and rapid deceleration capabilities.
3. Strength: Lower body strength (squats, deadlifts) is the foundation for power. Core strength provides stability in the air and against contact.
4. Conditioning: Basketball is primarily an anaerobic sport. Aerobic conditioning provides a base, but training should focus on high-intensity interval training (HIIT).

COURT-BASED CONDITIONING
- Suicides (Line Drills): Classic basketball conditioning, emphasizing acceleration and deceleration.
- 17s: Running the width of the court 17 times in roughly one minute. Tests anaerobic capacity.
- Slide Drills: Defensive shuffling side-to-side to build lateral endurance and glute strength.

PERIODIZATION
- Pre-season: High volume of strength and conditioning work to prepare for the season.
- In-season: Focus shifts to maintenance and injury prevention. Strength training frequency decreases to 1-2 times per week.

INJURY PREVENTION
- Ankle sprains and knee injuries (e.g., ACL tears) are common.
- Proprioceptive training (balance boards) and eccentric hamstring/quadriceps strengthening are recommended.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Focus conditioning on short, intense bursts rather than long slow distance running.
- Develop lower body strength to support jumping power and reduce injury risk.
- Maintain strength levels during the competitive season with short, intense workouts.
