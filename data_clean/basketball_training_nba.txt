SOURCE: NBA Academy - Strength and Conditioning for Young Players (2022)
ORGANIZATION: NBA Academy
CATEGORY: Basketball - Training
------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
The NBA Academy program focuses on long-term athletic development for youth and junior basketball players, prioritizing movement quality over maximal strength in the early stages.

FOUNDATIONAL MOVEMENTS
Players must master basic movement patterns before adding significant external load:
1. Squat (Goblet squats before barbell squats)
2. Hinge (Romanian deadlifts, glute bridges)
3. Push (Push-ups, overhead presses)
4. Pull (Inverted rows, pull-ups)
5. Lunge (Multi-directional lunges for court mobility)
6. Carry (Farmer's walks for core stability)

DEVELOPING EXPLOSIVENESS
- Olympic weightlifting derivatives (e.g., jump shrugs, clean pulls) are used to develop power.
- Medicine ball throws (chest passes, rotational throws) mimic the explosive upper-body actions of passing and rebounding.

MOBILITY AND FLEXIBILITY
- Basketball players often develop tight hips and ankles due to the nature of the sport and footwear.
- Daily mobility routines focusing on ankle dorsiflexion, hip internal/external rotation, and thoracic spine mobility are required.

MANAGING GROWING ATHLETES
- Growth spurts can temporarily reduce coordination and increase injury risk (e.g., Osgood-Schlatter disease).
- During rapid growth phases, training load should be monitored carefully, and jumping volume may need to be reduced.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Master fundamental movement patterns before lifting heavy weights.
- Prioritize ankle and hip mobility to counteract the demands of the sport.
- Monitor youth athletes carefully during growth spurts to prevent overuse injuries.
