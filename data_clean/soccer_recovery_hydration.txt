OVERVIEW
Hydration is one of the most directly impactful and most commonly neglected performance factors in team sports. Even mild dehydration (1–2% of body weight) measurably reduces physical and cognitive performance. This GSSI document summarizes hydration guidelines for team sport athletes, with specific applications for soccer.

WHY HYDRATION MATTERS
- Dehydration of just 1% body weight reduces aerobic capacity
- Dehydration of 2% impairs decision-making, reaction time, and skill execution
- Core body temperature rises faster when dehydrated, increasing heat illness risk
- Soccer players lose 1–2.5 liters of sweat per hour depending on heat and intensity

SWEAT RATE AND ELECTROLYTE LOSS
- Average sweat rate: 1–2 L/hour (higher in hot or humid conditions)
- Sweat contains sodium (primary), potassium, chloride, and magnesium
- Sodium loss: 500–1,500 mg per liter of sweat
- Replacing sodium is essential — plain water alone can cause hyponatremia (low blood sodium) if consumed in excessive quantities without electrolytes

HYDRATION RECOMMENDATIONS FOR SOCCER PLAYERS

1. Before Training / Match
 - Urine should be pale yellow (like lemonade) — not clear, not dark
 - Drink 400–600 mL of water or sports drink 2 hours before
 - Drink an additional 200 mL right before kickoff

2. During Training / Match
 - Take advantage of every stoppage, corner kick, and halftime to hydrate
 - Aim for 150–250 mL every 15–20 minutes
 - Use sports drinks (6–8% carbohydrate + electrolytes) for sessions >60 minutes
 - In hot weather, increase fluid intake further

3. After Training / Match
 - Weigh yourself before and after training
 - Drink 1.5 liters of fluid for every 1 kg of body weight lost
 - Include sodium (sports drink, salty snack, broth) to speed rehydration and retain fluids
 - Full rehydration typically takes 4–6 hours

SPORTS DRINKS vs. WATER
- Water is sufficient for sessions < 60 minutes in mild conditions
- Sports drinks are superior for sessions > 60 minutes because they:
 - Provide carbohydrates for energy
 - Contain electrolytes that enhance fluid absorption and retention
 - Taste better, which encourages athletes to drink more

SIGNS OF DEHYDRATION
- Dark urine
- Dry mouth, headache
- Muscle cramps
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Reduced concentration or irritability

HEAT-RELATED RISK MANAGEMENT
- Acclimatize to hot conditions over 7–14 days before competing in heat
- Pre-cooling strategies (cold towel, ice vest) reduce thermal strain before kickoff
- Recognize signs of heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, weakness, cold/pale clammy skin — stop activity immediately

PRACTICAL HYDRATION TIPS
- Bring your own labeled water bottle to every practice — do not share
- Start hydrating the day before a match, not just on match day
- Avoid excessive caffeine and alcohol (both are diuretics)
- Coffee in moderation (1–2 cups) does not cause dehydration at normal intake levels

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Dehydration starts impacting performance at just 1–2% body weight loss
- Drink 400–600 mL 2 hours before, 150–250 mL every 15–20 minutes during
- Replace 150% of fluid lost post-exercise (include electrolytes/sodium)
- Sports drinks are better than water for sessions >60 minutes
- Monitor urine color daily as a hydration check
