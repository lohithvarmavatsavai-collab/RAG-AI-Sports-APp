SOURCE: ATP/WTA Physical Preparation Standards and Testing Protocols (2023)
ORGANIZATION: Association of Tennis Professionals (ATP) / Women's Tennis Association (WTA)
CATEGORY: Tennis - Training
------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
The ATP and WTA jointly fund sports science research and establish physical preparation benchmarks for professional tennis. This document adapts their standards for club and intermediate-level players who aspire to train with professional methods.

PHYSICAL TESTING BENCHMARKS (ATP/WTA Benchmarks, adapted for club level)
- 5m Sprint: < 1.05 seconds (elite); < 1.20 seconds (good club standard)
- 10m Sprint: < 1.75 seconds (elite); < 2.00 seconds (good club standard)
- Change of Direction (COD — 505 test): < 2.3 seconds (elite); < 2.6 seconds (good club)
- Aerobic Capacity (Yo-Yo IRT Level 1): > 2,000 m (elite); > 1,200 m (good club)
- Maximal Strength (Back Squat 1RM): > 1.5x body weight (elite male); > 1.2x (good club male)

POSITION-SPECIFIC PHYSICAL DEMANDS
- Baseline players (e.g., Rafa Nadal style): Require exceptional lateral endurance, lower body strength and stability, and ability to sustain high-intensity rallies for extended periods. More aerobic conditioning needed.
- Serve-and-volley / all-court players: Require explosive first-step quickness, shoulder stability, and anaerobic power for short bursts.

ATP/WTA PHYSICAL PREPARATION PILLARS
1. Movement Efficiency
 - Efficient movement = covering court with fewer steps. Train using shadow footwork drills.
 - Split-step timing: The split-step (small hop as opponent hits) is the most important movement skill in tennis. Must be automatic.
 - First 3 steps determine 80% of court positioning — explosive acceleration drills are critical.

2. On-Court Conditioning
 - Ghosting: Moving to all court positions (forehand, backhand, volley, overhead, wide forehand, wide backhand) without a ball. Trains movement patterns and conditioning simultaneously.
 - Spider Drill with Racket: Combines ball pickup and directional movement for functional conditioning.

3. Strength and Power Training
 - Periodize strength work: Heavy strength (3–5 reps, 3–5 sets) during off-season; Power focus (3–5 reps at 50–70% 1RM, explosive intent) during pre-season; Maintenance (2 sessions/week) in-season.
 - Essential exercises: Bulgarian split squats, Romanian deadlifts, cable rotations, landmine press, single-arm cable rows.

4. Injury Prevention Focus Areas
 - Rotator cuff: External rotation, Y-T-W exercises with bands. Essential for all tennis players.
 - Hip flexors and adductors: Hip 90/90 mobility, Copenhagen plank.
 - Ankles: Single-leg balance progressions, resistance band eversion exercises.

TRAINING LOAD PERIODIZATION (Weekly Template — In-Season)
- Monday: Rest or light cross-training
- Tuesday: Tennis (2 hrs) + Lower body strength
- Wednesday: Tennis (2 hrs) + Core and stability
- Thursday: On-court conditioning (ghosting, suicide drills) + Upper body strength
- Friday: Match practice / sparring
- Saturday: Competition
- Sunday: Active recovery

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- The split-step and first 3 explosive steps are the most critical movement skills to develop.
- Use ghosting drills for sport-specific conditioning without match pressure.
- Bulgarian split squats and cable rotations are the most important strength exercises for tennis.
- Periodize strength from heavy (off-season) to powerful (pre-season) to maintenance (in-season).
