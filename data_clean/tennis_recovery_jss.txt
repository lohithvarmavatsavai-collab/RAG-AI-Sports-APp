SOURCE: Recovery and Regeneration Practices in Elite Tennis (2022)
ORGANIZATION: Journal of Sports Sciences (JSS)
CATEGORY: Tennis - Recovery
------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
Tennis tournaments often require players to compete on consecutive days, sometimes playing both singles and doubles. Effective recovery is paramount to maintaining performance and minimizing injury risk throughout a tournament.

EVIDENCE-BASED RECOVERY MODALITIES
1. Sleep: The foundation of recovery. 8-10 hours is recommended for athletes. Sleep extension before a tournament can improve performance.
2. Cold Water Immersion (CWI): Ice baths (10-15°C for 10-15 minutes) are highly effective at reducing delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS) and perceived fatigue, particularly during tournaments.
3. Active Recovery: 10-15 minutes of light aerobic exercise (e.g., stationary bike) post-match helps clear metabolic byproducts.
4. Compression Garments: Wearing lower-body compression garments during travel or sleep can aid in reducing swelling and improving venous return.
5. Massage/Foam Rolling: Helps alleviate muscle tightness and improve range of motion. Should be focused on the legs, lower back, and hitting shoulder.

TIMING OF RECOVERY
- 0-30 min post-match: Cool down, rehydrate, consume recovery snack (carbs + protein).
- 30-60 min post-match: CWI or contrast baths, stretching/foam rolling.
- 1-3 hours post-match: Full meal, massage.
- Evening: High-quality sleep.

PSYCHOLOGICAL RECOVERY
- Mental fatigue significantly impairs physical performance.
- Strategies like mindfulness, reading, or listening to music can help players 'switch off' from competition stress.

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Prioritize sleep above all other recovery strategies.
- Use cold water immersion to manage soreness during tournaments.
- Establish a consistent post-match recovery routine.
