SOURCE: Pre-match and Match-day Nutrition for Soccer Players (2022)
ORGANIZATION: FIFA Medical Assessment and Research Centre (F-MARC)
CATEGORY: Soccer - Nutrition
------------------------------------------------------------

OVERVIEW
FIFA's F-MARC nutrition guidelines provide evidence-based recommendations for soccer players at all levels. Proper nutrition fuels performance, supports recovery, and reduces injury risk. This document focuses on pre-match, during-match, and post-match nutrition.

CARBOHYDRATE REQUIREMENTS
- Soccer is a high-intensity, intermittent sport heavily reliant on carbohydrates (glycogen stores)
- Total carbohydrate intake on training days: 5–7 g/kg body weight
- On match days: 7–10 g/kg body weight (higher demand due to match intensity)
- Pre-match meal (3–4 hours before): 1–4 g/kg of low-fiber, easily digestible carbohydrates
 - Examples: white rice, pasta, bread, bananas, sports drinks
- During match (if >60 min): 30–60 g of carbohydrate per hour from drinks or gels
- Post-match: 1.0–1.5 g/kg body weight within the first 30–45 minutes

PROTEIN REQUIREMENTS
- Daily protein: 1.4–1.7 g/kg body weight for soccer players
- Protein is critical for muscle repair, especially post-match
- Post-match protein target: 20–25 g high-quality protein within 2 hours
 - Examples: chicken breast, Greek yogurt, milk, eggs, whey protein if needed

HYDRATION
- Pre-match: Drink 5–7 mL/kg body weight in the 2–4 hours before kickoff
- During match: Sip 150–250 mL every 15–20 minutes (use half-time and stoppages)
- Post-match: Replace 150% of fluid lost (weigh before and after, drink 1.5L per kg of weight lost)

FAT AND MICRONUTRIENTS
- Fat intake should not be excessive before matches (slows digestion)
- Iron, calcium, and vitamin D are commonly insufficient in female soccer players
- Antioxidant-rich foods (vegetables, fruits) support recovery

COMMON NUTRITION MISTAKES
- Skipping pre-match meal or eating too close to kickoff (< 1 hour)
- Relying only on water without carbohydrate replacement during long matches
- Not eating within the post-match recovery window
- Over-relying on protein while neglecting carbohydrates

PRACTICAL SAMPLE MEAL PLAN (Match Day)
- 7:00am Breakfast (4 hrs before 11am match): Oatmeal with banana, toast with jam, 500 mL water
- 9:30am Pre-match snack: Small granola bar, 250 mL sports drink
- During match: 500 mL sports drink at halftime
- Post-match: Chocolate milk + chicken sandwich + 750 mL water
- Evening: Pasta with lean protein and vegetables

KEY TAKEAWAYS
- Carbohydrates are the primary fuel for soccer; do not restrict them
- Protein supports recovery; eat some protein after every training or match
- Hydration affects performance and cognitive function significantly
- Timing matters — eat at the right times, not just the right foods
